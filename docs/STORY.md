# The Quiet Under Brackenfell (main story)

All of it happens inside the map we already have: the town, the wharf, the chapel crypt, the Old Mine in the south-west cliffs, the Old
Watchtower, the lake and the inn's cellar. No new ground.

## Premise
Fifty-one winters ago the miners of the Old Mine broke through into a drowned cavern under Mirrormere and found silver that hums when
you hold it near your ear. The ones who stayed down went quiet. They would not climb back to the light, they would not speak, and they
took what hung from other men's belts. The founders (young Rowan among them, the Sexton, the first Warden) sealed the mine with the
Sexton's key and made a bargain nobody wrote down: every dawn the bell rings and bread is left in the cave on the lake shore, reached by
the old smugglers' tunnel from the Honeyed Hart's cellar. The bell, the bread and the silence kept the Hollow below.
This midsummer the bell rope rotted and the bread stopped. Corwin the miner went in alone after the silver. The Hollow are hungry.

## Characters
- **Warden Hale** (gate): knows half, hates it; he barred the door and never asked what was behind it.
- **Elder Rowan**: knows all; was a boy at the sealing. Has been leaving the bread himself since the Sexton died, and stopped when his hips gave out.
- **Old Maren**: reads the dead. **Brother Aldous**: keeps the Sexton's ledger without understanding it. **Finn Reedwater**: found the man on the wharf.
- **Dunstan Pell**, the drowned miner: first of the Hollow to try to leave. **The Foreman**: the one who still remembers shifts and still counts heads.
- **Corwin**: not dead. He is the last one who can speak.

## Act 1: Dry Lungs (built)
1. **The wharf.** Hale at the gate, in his first breath, mentions a man on the shingle at the wharf. Under a sailcloth: a miner's tally token
   (OM-14), silver in his teeth, lungs with no lake in them. A wet note: "Tell Rowan the bread did not come."
2. **Maren** reads the token: Dunstan Pell, a miner who went down with Corwin's crew. Starved, walked a long way in the dark. Says the Sexton kept the mine key.
3. **The crypt.** The Sexton's Key quest (existing) now ends with the Sexton's ledger and the iron mine key in the reliquary.
4. **The Old Mine.** The door opens. A timbered adit, a flooded gallery, a collapsed stope with a way round, the deep chamber. Delvers rush, Lurkers wait in the dark,
   the Foreman guards a silver-lit hollow. Corwin's journal, a unique weapon, and a second exit that comes out in the cliffs above the Hermit's Pool.
5. **Hale.** Bring the journal: the bread, the cave on the lake, the tunnel from the inn's cellar. End of Act 1.

## Act 2: The Bread Road (to build)
The tunnel from the Hart's cellar to the lake cave; the Old Watchtower (guard room, cells, flooded vault) where the first Warden's records and the
silver lamp are kept. Rowan is confronted. Twist: the Hollow are not monsters; they are the miners, and Corwin among them, kept like livestock for
fifty years so that the village could keep its quiet. Hale has known the bread was for people.

## Act 3: The Choice (to build)
Corwin gives you the lamp that quiets them, or the key that lets them out.
- **Seal it again.** Hale and Rowan keep the pact, the bell rings every dawn, a new grave in the churchyard (Corwin's), the square is as it was.
- **Open it.** The Hollow are fed, clothed and walk up into the square at dawn; the bell tolls twice; a banner of the miners' guild hangs on the Hart; Rowan steps down.

## Rules of the story
Told through people, notes and places; no text dumps. Every step sends you somewhere new with a reason and nothing dead-ends if done out of order
(the key can be found before the token; Maren can be asked before the body is seen).
