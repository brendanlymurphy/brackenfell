# Brackenfell town plan (the design authority for how the town looks and is laid out)

Written at Build 69 after the owner's verdict: "everything is blocky, blocked off, clunky and lazy, so copy paste. Think like an
artist and a city planner. Every touch intentional, not just filling space, but all the space filled at the same time, and full
of life." Nothing new is added by this plan: the same buildings, people and places, re-sited and finished. Where this file and a
brief disagree, this file wins. Proof is always two pictures: one from straight above, and eye height along the route a player walks.

## 1. What is wrong (seen from above, Build 69: shots top0/top1/top2)

1. **Houses stand on a lawn.** Each is a rectangle at its own random angle with grass on all four sides. Nothing defines a street:
   the roads are tan stripes painted through grass, and the houses do not face them. This is the root of "copy paste": identical
   objects scattered, not a place that grew.
2. **The square is a grey disc.** Cobbles end in a round hard edge with grass between them and the buildings. No frontage encloses it.
3. **No plots.** No yard, fence, garden, shed or back gate belongs to any house. The space between houses is nobody's.
4. **No edge.** The town fades into the same lawn it stands on. There is no wall line, ditch or gate that says inside and outside.
5. **Flat.** One height, one ground texture per zone. The one raised piece (the upper ward terrace) is a box dropped in a field.
6. **One roof.** Nearly every roof is the same blue-grey slate at the same pitch, so from any height the town is one colour.
7. **No hierarchy.** Every road is the same width and surface; no street is "the" street; no view ends on anything.

## 2. Principles (what a town that grew looks like)

- **Buildings make the streets.** A house's front wall stands on the street line. Its side walls touch the neighbours' (a party
  wall) or leave a deliberate gap: a ginnel 1.2 to 1.8 m wide, or a cart gate into a yard. Frontage lines bend and step a little;
  they are never a ruler line and never a scatter. No house is rotated for variety: its angle comes from its street.
- **Every house has a plot.** Behind each frontage a long narrow plot runs back to a back lane or the wall: a yard fenced or
  walled from its neighbours, holding what that household would own: woodstore, privy, washing line, kitchen bed, hen run, pig
  sty, a workshop or drying rack for the trade, a back gate. No two plots dressed alike; each tells who lives there.
- **No lawn inside the town.** Every square metre inside the edge is one of: street, market place, yard, garden, churchyard, green
  with a purpose (a drying green, the archery butts), or water. Grass appears only where people leave it: at wall feet, in
  churchyards, on the green, along the back lanes.
- **Hierarchy.** One main street runs from the bridge on the east to the west gate and swells into the market place. Chapel
  Street climbs north from the market to the chapel. Wharf Street falls south to the lake. Back lanes run behind the plots.
  Ginnels link them. Widths: main street 6 to 7 m, side streets 4 m, back lanes 2.5 m, ginnels 1.5 m.
- **The ground falls to the water.** The chapel stands on the highest ground at the north, the lake is the low point at the
  south, the river bank drops at the east. About 5 m of fall across the town, most of it in two places: the rise of Chapel
  Street (a few broad steps and a ramp) and the drop to the wharf. Streets are crowned with a drain; plots step down with low
  retaining walls. The upper ward becomes part of this slope, not a box: a walled court on a real shoulder of ground.
- **An edge.** A palisade on a bank with a ditch, following the ground, encloses the town from the river round to the lake; gates
  where the main street and the chapel road pass through. Houses back onto it; lean-tos use it. Outside it: fields, orchards,
  hedged closes. The few buildings outside (mill, farm, hunter, fisher, tower) are outliers with their own closes.
- **Views end on something.** From each gate a landmark is in sight (bell tower, chapel, the inn's sign and gable). Streets bend so
  the eye meets a building, an arch or a tree, never open sky at the end of a straight.
- **Materials tell the district.** Paving, walling and roofing change by district (section 3) and by wealth, so a player could
  say where they are from a photograph.
- **Every object has an owner and a reason.** A barrel stands where a cooper, an inn or a gutter needs it. Nothing is placed to
  fill; a space that needs filling gets a use first (whose is it, what do they do there) and is dressed for that use.
- **Life follows the plan.** People are where the plan says work happens: the yard, the stall, the well, the wash place, the
  quay. Animals are in the plots that keep them. (Characters and routines are other workers' files; the plan names the places.)

## 4. Order of work

One district is finished completely (plan, ground, edges, plots, house fronts, dressing, people's places) before the next begins,
so there is always one place that shows what "done" means. Order: **Market Place** (the benchmark), Wharf Street and Wharfside,
Chapel Street and Chapelgate, Riverside, Westgate, Upper Ward, then the edge all round.

### The benchmark: Market Place

- Shape: an irregular four-sided place, wider at the inn end, about 34 x 26 m, where the main street swells. Not a disc.
- North side: the inn, full width, with its gallery. East side: the store and the mouth of the shop alley (the alley is the main
  street continuing to the bridge). West side: the smithy, open-fronted to the place, with the main street leaving beside it
  toward the west gate. South side: three or four of the existing houses brought to the line as a row, with the mouth of Wharf
  Street between them and a ginnel.
- Every building on the place stands on its line and touches its neighbour or a gateway. Cobbles run to the wall feet.
- Furniture with reasons: the well where carts can reach it, the market cross on the axis seen from the bridge, stalls in two
  rows leaving a cart way, the pillory by the guard's corner, the bonfire place clear of roofs.
- Behind each front: its plot, dressed for its household, reached by a ginnel or cart gate.
- Acceptance: from above, no grass touches a building on the place and no house stands alone; at eye height from the four
  entries the place reads as a room with walls; every existing door, quest spot, secret, stall keeper and route still works.

## 5. Rules for whoever builds this

- Re-site, do not invent: the same buildings and people. A house may move, turn to its street, and share a wall. Its interior
  cell, its door, its household and anything a quest or secret pins to it move with it (search for its id and for literal
  coordinates near it before moving anything; the story, secrets, routes, npc and bot steps must pass).
- The infill placer (`55b_infill.js`) scatters by test-and-accept: replace scatter with authored street lines and plots.
- Do not exceed the budget views by more than 3%. Plots are fences, low walls and a few props: cheap.
- Show the picture from above and the eye-height walk, before and after, for every block. Look at them before reporting.
